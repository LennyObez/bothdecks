# Code of conduct

## The short version

Be someone people want to work with. Assume the other person is competent and acting in good faith. Argue about
the code, never about the person writing it.

## What is expected

- **Critique the work, not its author.** "This query runs on every request" is a review. "You clearly did not
  think about this" is not.
- **Say what you actually verified.** A claim without the command that produced it wastes everyone's time and
  occasionally sends someone down a false trail for a day.
- **Accept correction.** Being wrong in public is the normal cost of working in the open. Nobody here loses
  standing by saying so.
- **Give people room.** Contributors work in different time zones, in second or third languages, and around
  the rest of their lives. Silence is usually a schedule, not an insult.

## What is not acceptable

Harassment of any kind. Personal attacks. Demeaning comments about who someone is rather than what they wrote.
Sustained disruption of a discussion. Publishing someone's private information. Unwelcome sexual attention.

This project exists to help people find work, and a share of its users are in a precarious position. Anyone
contributing to it should hold themselves to the standard the product asks of its own users.

## Scope

This applies in the repository, in its issues, pull requests and discussions, and anywhere someone represents
the project.

## Reporting

Report a problem privately to the maintainer through https://github.com/LennyObez. Reports are handled
confidentially. Consequences range from a request to change behaviour, through temporary exclusion from a
discussion, to a permanent ban, proportionate to what happened and whether it continued after being raised.

The maintainer is subject to this document on the same terms as everyone else.
